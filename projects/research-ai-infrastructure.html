The engineering problem

A research assistant can sound confident while overlooking conflicting documents, missing support, or incorrect attribution. Research AI Infrastructure explores how to make these failure conditions visible before a finding is accepted.

The central design goal is to reject unsupported conclusions while preserving answers that the available evidence does support.

My engineering focus

The work covers research-tooling architecture, bounded offline evaluation, evidence-handling rules, and integration contracts. It treats an answer as something that needs support and a scope, rather than treating fluent output as sufficient.

  • Support: tie a conclusion to source material and expose missing coverage.
  • Conflict: preserve meaningful disagreement instead of merging it into false certainty.
  • Attribution: keep source identity and transformations inspectable.
  • Authority: separate findings from permission to invoke tools or change another system.
  • Replaceability: avoid coupling the enduring evidence to a single model or provider.

The design tradeoff

A system that releases every plausible answer risks unsupported claims. A system that holds every uncertain answer loses useful information. Evaluation has to distinguish those outcomes: valid answers should survive while missing support and material conflicts remain visible.

That makes negative cases as useful as successful ones. The question is not just whether an answer can be produced, but whether the system can explain why that answer is supported or why a conclusion should remain open.

Why it matters for security analysis

Analysts make similar judgments about alerts: what is observed, what is inferred, which sources disagree, and what additional context would change the decision? RAI contributes engineering patterns for assistance that keeps those distinctions explicit.

The public analyst workflow shows the corresponding investigation method. It is a documented process supported by controlled labs, not evidence of autonomous SOC operation.

Evaluation cases that matter

Successful outputs are only part of the evaluation story. Missing coverage, contradictory observations, and mismatched attribution are important cases to make visible. The design question is whether a conclusion keeps the support it needs.

The public evidence-coverage engine demonstrates a narrow version of that idea using two training records. It computes a hold for an omitted source or a mismatched value, and preserves both readings when coverage passes. It checks structured attribution, not source truth or arbitrary prose; it is not the private RAI engine.

Public scope

This is a high-level account of private architecture and implementation work. It does not claim a production security agent, independently measured autonomous reliability, real-world security efficacy, or a replacement for human review. Private prompts, permissions, configuration, and research inputs remain excluded.
