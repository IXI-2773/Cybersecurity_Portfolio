Kali VM & Network Visibility

Completed hands-on experience connecting isolated attack activity to packet evidence, IDS alerts and analyst review.

Kali Linux · VMware · Nmap · Wireshark · Snort · Splunk

Completed lab experience: attack simulation, 5,000+ packets reviewed and Snort rule tuning.

My completed lab experience

I used an isolated VMware environment with Kali Linux to simulate scanning, brute-force and exploitation activity against lab targets. I reviewed more than 5,000 captured packets in Wireshark, then implemented and tuned Snort rules for the simulated traffic.

The value of the exercise was seeing the same activity from both sides: what the Kali VM generated, what appeared on the network and what an analyst could support from the retained evidence.

How I worked through the network evidence

  1. Bound the virtual lab. Keep the Kali VM and target systems on an isolated network with known scope.
  2. Generate controlled activity. Use the lab to produce reconnaissance and attack-like traffic without targeting public systems.
  3. Inspect packets in Wireshark. Review protocols, conversations, ports and timing to understand what crossed the network.
  4. Tune Snort detections. Compare rule logic with the captured traffic and refine signals that were too broad or noisy.
  5. Carry the question into Splunk. Use available event context to organize the activity into an analyst-readable timeline rather than treating an alert as a conclusion.
